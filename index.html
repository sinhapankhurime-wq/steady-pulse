How the score is calculated

Every 5 seconds the app takes the last 64 seconds of beat-to-beat intervals, resamples them evenly at 2 Hz, removes the linear trend and computes the power spectrum. It finds the highest spectral peak between 0.04 and 0.26 Hz and sums the power in a ~0.03 Hz window around it.

ratio = (peak ÷ below) × (peak ÷ above)
score = ln(ratio + 1), shown 0–16

“Below” and “above” are the power on either side of the peak window, from 0 to 0.4 Hz. A smooth, wave-like heart rhythm puts most of its power into one peak, usually near 0.1 Hz (6 breaths per minute), and scores high. Points add up the 5-second scores over a session.

  • Method published by HeartMath: McCraty (2022), HeartCloud help, and Balaji et al., Scientific Reports (2025).
  • HeartMath’s exact code is proprietary, so scores follow theirs in direction and scale but will not match exactly.
  • Camera readings need a still hand. Movement and pressure changes are detected and skipped; if too much is skipped, no score is shown.
